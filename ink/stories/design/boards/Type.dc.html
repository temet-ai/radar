02 · cells & type

The terminal's face,
and others where they count.

The terminal's own font is radar's working face: every row, every key, every number in a column. Hierarchy inside it comes from colour slots, weight, case, spacing and the attributes a terminal knows. Where a moment deserves more, a title, a splash, a screen with nothing on it, Ink can bring in other faces: bigger text through kitty's text sizing, a display face drawn as a picture, and Unicode's own letterforms.

The cell

One column by one row; every layout is counted in them. Letters, box drawing and nerd icons take one cell. Emoji take two, so the emoji pack is the only one that is two wide, and its rows are laid out for it.

Ink measures each grapheme before it places it; a row that does not fit is cut with …, never in the middle of an icon or a wide glyph.

Three ways to bring in another face, each for a specific place. Every one falls back to the terminal face, bold.

Where: an item page's title, a dashboard head. The terminal draws the same font at two cells per cell, so it stays on the grid: two rows tall, two columns a letter.

Checked 2026-10-13: only kitty 0.40+ draws it. Ghostty parses it but does not draw it yet (issue #10333), and WezTerm does not (#7814). radar's output layer also drops the sequence today. Elsewhere the title is one bold row. Parked.

A display face

drawn as a picture
Nothing needs you. no reviews, no red pipelines

Where: the splash, an empty screen, a done-for-the-day moment. Ink sets the line in Instrument Serif italic, draws it to a PNG and places it as a kitty image a whole number of cells wide.

No image support: the same words in bold, one row.

Where: section labels in small caps, a brand line, a sign-off. They are characters, so they go everywhere text goes.

Risky: most monospace and Nerd fonts lack the mathematical letters, so they fall back to another font at a different width, and screen readers spell them out. Small caps are safer but uneven. Decoration only, never content.

Display type

new in ink

For the one number a screen is about, draw it: a 3 × 5 digit in half blocks, three rows tall, in the slot that says what it counts. Only on a dashboard head or an empty screen; never inside a list.
